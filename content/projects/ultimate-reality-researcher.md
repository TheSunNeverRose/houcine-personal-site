## What I'm trying to find out

Can an AI-assisted research process help me compare arguments about ultimate reality without getting stuck repeating its first conclusion?

The project is a research agent that works through philosophical and religious questions over multiple cycles. I'm interested in whether the process improves the quality of the investigation, not whether it can produce a confident-sounding answer.

## Why I started it

Questions about first causes, necessary reality, cosmology, and religious claims often depend on assumptions that need to be made explicit. I wanted a way to return to a question, keep track of the line of inquiry, and see which objections still need an answer.

## How I'm using AI

The model helps investigate questions and review arguments. I treat its output as material to examine, not as proof. A claim can sound coherent and still be wrong, poorly sourced, or missing an important objection.

## Things that can go wrong

- An argument may be presented unfairly or reduced to an easy target.
- A conclusion may not actually follow from the premises.
- The system may repeat an earlier claim without adding evidence.
- Its confidence may be out of proportion to the argument.
- A serious counterargument may be overlooked.

Running more research cycles doesn't guarantee better answers. It can just mean the same mistake gets repeated, so checking sources and objections matters.

## Where the project stands

The conclusions are still provisional. I haven't established the truth of a religion or arrived at a final theory of reality. I'm working on making the research process easier to inspect and on separating source-backed claims from the model's own reasoning.
