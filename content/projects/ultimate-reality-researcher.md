## The question

Can an AI-assisted research workflow help investigate competing claims about ultimate reality in a way that is structured, critical, and honest about uncertainty?

This project explores that question through a research agent designed to examine philosophical and religious arguments rather than simply produce a single confident answer.

## Why I am working on it

Questions about first causes, necessary reality, cosmology, and the truth of religious claims are difficult partly because they involve different kinds of evidence and assumptions. I wanted a way to pursue those questions over multiple research cycles, preserve the direction of the investigation, and see whether conclusions become better supported or merely repeated.

## Approach

The project uses an AI model as a research assistant. The workflow is iterative: investigate a question, assess arguments, track provisional conclusions, and continue examining unresolved points. The model's output is not treated as independent proof. Its claims still need scrutiny, source checking, and philosophical evaluation.

## What I am watching for

- Whether arguments are represented fairly rather than reduced to easy targets.
- Whether a conclusion follows from the stated premises.
- Whether the same claim is being repeated without new evidence.
- Whether confidence is calibrated to the quality of the argument.
- Whether alternative explanations and unresolved objections remain visible.

## Limitations

An AI system can produce fluent but unsupported claims, overlook relevant objections, misread sources, and become anchored to an earlier conclusion. More cycles do not automatically mean more truth. A research process needs explicit checks against these failure modes.

The project's conclusions remain provisional. This page does not claim that it has established the truth of a religion or a final metaphysical theory.

## Next steps

Improve how the project tracks evidence and objections, distinguish source-backed claims from model-generated reasoning, and make the research process easier for another person to inspect and criticise.
