## The starting point

This is a made-up local service idea for practising business planning. I haven't launched it, taken orders, or checked whether people would pay for it.

Before getting attached to the idea, I'd want to be able to answer a few plain questions:

- **Customer:** Who would use it, and when?
- **Problem:** What do they do at the moment? What's frustrating or expensive about that?
- **Offer:** What exactly would I provide?
- **Finding customers:** How would the first few people hear about it?
- **Delivery:** What equipment, time, skills, and recurring costs would it take?

If those answers are vague, a spreadsheet won't make the idea any more real.

## The basic maths

For a simple service, the money left from each sale after variable costs is:

`contribution per sale = price charged − variable cost per sale`

If that contribution is positive, a rough break-even estimate is:

`break-even sales = fixed costs ÷ contribution per sale`

That's only a starting calculation. It leaves out taxes, paying myself, refunds, seasonal changes, and the possibility that I couldn't handle the number of jobs the estimate assumes.

## Facts, estimates, and guesses

If I build a model for a real idea, I'd label each number. Some might come from a quote or a published source. Others would be estimates. The rest would be guesses that need checking. I don't want a spreadsheet to make a guess look like a fact just because it has two decimal places.

## What would make me drop it?

If potential customers aren't interested, finding them costs too much, or each sale loses money at a realistic price, I'd need to change the idea or leave it alone. That's useful to know early.

The next step is to choose a specific service, get realistic cost estimates, and talk to potential customers without leading their answers. Until I've done that, this is only a practice exercise.
