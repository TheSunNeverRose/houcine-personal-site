## The question

Could a Tunisian olive-oil producer find a place in Singapore? I chose a real product and a real destination so I could work with evidence instead of a country-level checklist. This is a first-pass desk study, not a recommendation to export.

I used an AI assistant to help locate and organise public sources. The source links are included so the figures can be checked directly. I have not interviewed Singaporean buyers, contacted an importer, audited supermarket shelves, or obtained shipping quotes.

## What the numbers say

The World Bank's WITS page, using UN Comtrade data, reports Singapore's 2024 imports of olive oil under HS 1509 at 2,795,220 kg worth US$25.50 million. The figures below are reported values unless marked as a calculation.

| Item | Finding | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singapore, total imports in 2024 | 2,795,220 kg; US$25.50 million | A real import market exists, but this total includes different olive-oil products and does not tell me what any one segment will pay. |
| Spain | 1,704,470 kg; US$15.83 million | About 61.0% of reported import volume. |
| Italy | 884,710 kg; US$7.44 million | About 31.6% of reported import volume. |
| Spain and Italy together | About 92.6% of volume | Calculated from the reported quantities. This suggests a market with established suppliers, not an easy opening. |
| Tunisia | 130 kg; about US$1,800 | A very small recorded flow in this dataset, around 0.0046% of total volume. It does not prove that Tunisian oil could not sell there. |
| Average import value | About US$9.12 per kg overall | Calculation: total declared value divided by total quantity. This is not a retail price or a supplier quote. |
| Tunisia's recorded unit value | About US$13.85 per kg | Calculation from only 130 kg. The quantity is too small to use as a sensible pricing benchmark. |

Source: [WITS / UN Comtrade, Singapore olive-oil imports in 2024](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/SGP/year/2024/tradeflow/Imports/partner/ALL/product/1509). Figures and percentages are rounded. The page uses HS 1509, so the figures should not be treated as an extra-virgin-only market estimate.

## Regulatory facts to check early

Singapore's Food Agency says businesses importing processed food for commercial sale must be registered with SFA and must obtain the relevant import permit through TradeNet. Importers also need to meet Singapore's food rules. Before making a shipment, the exact product category, code, labelling and supporting documents should be confirmed for the oil and packaging in question.

Source: [Singapore Food Agency, import of food for commercial sale](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-import-export/commercial-imports/what-you-need-to-know-for-import-of-food).

Singapore's IRAS explains that import GST is 9% of the applicable taxable value, which includes the cost, insurance and freight value and any customs duties payable. A real landed-cost model would need a freight quote, the correct customs classification, importer charges and advice on how GST applies to the business. The percentage alone is not the total cost of entry.

Source: [IRAS, importing goods](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-%28gst%29/claiming-gst-%28input-tax%29/importing-of-goods).

## What I can conclude so far

The broad question, "Does Singapore import olive oil?", has a clear answer: yes. The next question is harder. The 2024 data show a market dominated by Spanish and Italian supply and almost no recorded Tunisian volume. That is a reason to investigate the barrier to entry, not evidence on its own that the idea will succeed or fail.

I would not recommend entering the market yet. The next useful work would be a small shelf-price comparison, two or three conversations with importers, a supplier specification for a defined Tunisian product, and a landed-cost estimate based on real shipping and importer quotes. Until then, there is no defensible selling price or margin to present.
