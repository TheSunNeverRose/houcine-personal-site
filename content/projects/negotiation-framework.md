## Before the conversation

Can preparing a few notes help me think more clearly during a negotiation? This is a framework I've put together from general ideas about negotiation. I haven't tested it in a documented real-world trial yet.

## My checklist

### 1. Decide what I need

Write down the outcome I'm hoping for and why it matters. Separate the things I genuinely need from preferences I could trade away.

### 2. Check what I know about the other person

List their likely priorities, but mark assumptions as assumptions. Think of questions that could help me understand their constraints.

### 3. Think beyond the headline term

Price or timing may not be the only things that can change. Scope, support, responsibilities, flexibility, and payment structure can matter too.

### 4. Consider the no-deal option

What happens if we don't agree? Knowing the realistic alternative makes it easier to judge an offer instead of accepting it just to finish the conversation.

### 5. Review it afterwards

Afterward, I'd write down what happened, what surprised me, and which assumptions turned out to be wrong.

## Where this checklist falls short

Preparation can't replace experience or knowledge of the subject. It also can't fix a situation where the other side has much more power or is negotiating in bad faith. The point is simply to arrive with a clearer idea of what matters.

I'd like to try it before a low-stakes conversation and revise it based on what actually helps. Until then, it's a draft, not a proven method.
