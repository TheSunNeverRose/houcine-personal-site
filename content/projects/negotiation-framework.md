## The question

Can a short preparation routine make a negotiation more deliberate and less reactive?

This is a personal framework assembled from general reasoning about negotiation. It has not been validated through a formal study or a documented real-world trial.

## The framework

### 1. Define the outcome

Write down the result I want and the reason it matters. Separate the essential requirements from preferences that could be traded.

### 2. Map interests

List what the other side may care about, but mark guesses as guesses. Prepare questions that could clarify their constraints rather than assuming I already understand them.

### 3. Generate options

Look for variables beyond the obvious headline term: timing, scope, support, flexibility, certainty, responsibilities, or payment structure. Different priorities can create room for an agreement.

### 4. Know the alternative

Consider what happens if there is no agreement. A realistic alternative helps prevent accepting a poor arrangement simply to avoid an uncomfortable conversation.

### 5. Review the result

After the conversation, record what happened, which assumptions were wrong, and what I would prepare differently next time.

## Limitations

The framework is not a substitute for domain knowledge, legal advice, or professional negotiation training. It may be insufficient when power is highly unequal, the stakes are serious, or the other party acts in bad faith. A checklist cannot guarantee a fair or successful outcome.

## Next step

Use it to prepare for a low-stakes conversation, then revise the sheet based on what was actually useful. Until that test is done, it should be treated as a working tool rather than a proven method.
